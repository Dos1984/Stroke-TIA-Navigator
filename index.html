Stroke Mimics — Differentiating Features
Mimics account for up to a third of "suspected strokes." Recognising them prevents inappropriate thrombolysis — but uncertainty should never delay urgent referral when stroke remains plausible.
Seizure / Todd's Paresis
Commonest mimic (~13–23%). Post-ictal focal weakness or dysphasia can last hours to days. Look for a witnessed seizure, tongue-biting, incontinence, or a known epilepsy history.
Hypoglycaemia
Always check glucose first — neuroglycopenia can produce pure focal deficits that resolve completely with correction.
Migraine With Aura
Gradual march of visual/sensory symptoms over minutes, headache, prior similar episodes. Hemiplegic migraine is a recognised, less common mimic.
Sepsis / Metabolic (e.g. Hyponatraemia)
"Recrudescence" — decompensation of an old lesion. Systemically unwell; deficits resolve as the metabolic derangement is corrected.
Syncope
Brief loss of consciousness, rapid full recovery, no persisting focal signs.
Functional Neurological Disorder
Inconsistent exam findings — e.g. a positive Hoover's sign (involuntary hip extension when the "good" leg is asked to flex against resistance).

Bamford / Oxford Classifier (Ischaemic Stroke)
Bedside classification using presenting signs, before imaging is available. Check the features present — the likely subtype updates live. Once CT confirms pathology, add: I = infarct, H = haemorrhage, S = syndrome (pre-imaging).
